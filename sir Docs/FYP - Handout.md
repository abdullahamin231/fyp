## **FYP Supervision Handout** 

Idea Selection and Early Proposal Guidance 

This handout is for FYP groups at the idea-generation stage. Before thinking about implementation, your first job is to identify a project idea that is **novel** , **substantial** , **defensible** , and **feasible within the FYP timeline** . 

## **1. What matters most** 

At the start, your idea will be judged mainly on two things: 

- **Novelty** 

- **Scope** 

Of these two, **novelty is more important** . Your idea must clearly answer: 

- What new are you doing? 

- What is your contribution? 

- How is your work meaningfully different from what already exists? 

## **2. What does not count as novelty** 

The following are usually **not enough** on their own: 

- We are using AI or LLMs. 

- We are using APIs, libraries, or toolkits. 

- This exists globally, but we are making it for the local market. 

- We are building a chatbot for a specific domain. 

- We are combining common features into one platform. 

- We are improving the UI of an existing idea. 

These may describe the project, but they do not clearly define a contribution. 

## **3. What may count as novelty** 

- A meaningful technical improvement 

- A better workflow or system design 

- Solving a specific weakness in existing tools 

- Addressing a neglected subproblem 

- Introducing a strong evaluation or validation approach 

A good idea is not just an app. It is a project with a **clear contribution** . 

## **4. Start with the problem, not the app** 

Do not start with ideas such as “Let’s make a chatbot,” “Let’s build an AI app,” or “Let’s make a management system.” Start with the problem instead: 

- Who has a problem? 

- What exactly is the problem? 

- What already exists? 

- 

- Why is that not enough? 

- What can we contribute that is meaningfully different? 

- 

## **Useful framing:** 

“X users struggle with Y problem in Z context. Existing systems solve part of the problem, but fail in A, B, or C. Our project contributes D.” 

## **5. Generate multiple ideas first** 

Do not lock onto the first idea your group thinks of. Each group should generate multiple candidate ideas first. Weak ideas will be rejected. Better ones will be refined. That is normal. 

## **6. Questions every idea must answer** 

- What exact problem are we solving? 

- 

- Who faces this problem? 

- 

- What already exists? 

- 

- What do existing systems do well? 

- 

- What gap or limitation remains? 

- 

- What exactly are we contributing? 

- 

- Why is this large enough for a two-semester FYP? 

- 

- Can the core idea be prototyped by December? 

If your answer to **“What is your contribution?”** is weak, vague, or generic, the idea is not ready. 

## **7. Comparison with existing work is mandatory** 

For every serious idea, you must compare it with existing work such as commercial products, open-source systems, research tools, common off-the-shelf solutions, and similar student projects. 

|**Existing system/product**|**What it does well**|**Limitation/gap**|**Our contribution**|
|---|---|---|---|
|System A|...|...|...|
|System B|...|...|...|



This comparison helps answer the most important question: **Why is your project not just a copy?** 

## **8. Do not confuse features with contribution** 

A long feature list does **not** make a project strong. Items such as login/signup, dashboards, admin panel, notifications, profiles, reports, and a basic chat interface are usually **supporting features** , not the main contribution. 

## **9. Expect your idea to be challenged** 

Be ready for questions such as: 

- What is new here? 

- Why is this not a copy? 

- Why can this not be done with an existing API and a simple interface? 

- What is your actual contribution? 

- Why is this a Final Year Project and not a small semester project? 

- What will be implemented by December? 

## **10. Keep the timeline in mind** 

- **Proposal Defense:** defend the problem, gap, contribution, novelty, and scope 

- **By December:** core features should be implemented as a proof of concept / prototype 

- **By April:** the project should be mostly complete 

Your idea must be strong enough to survive Proposal Defense and practical enough to reach prototype stage on time. 

## **11. How to use LLMs properly** 

You may use LLMs to help with brainstorming ideas, identifying existing products, finding possible gaps, improving problem statements, refining contribution statements, and simulating likely questions. 

However, do not use LLMs to blindly choose trendy ideas. If an LLM suggests an idea, you still need to ask: Does this already exist? Is it too generic? What is actually new? Can we defend it? 

## **12. What to bring at the idea stage** 

Each group should come prepared with **multiple serious candidate ideas** , not just one. For each shortlisted idea, be ready to provide: 

1. Tentative title 

2. Problem statement 

3. Target users/stakeholders 

4. Existing related systems/products 

5. Why those are not enough 

6. Identified gap 

7. Proposed contribution 

8. Why the idea is novel 

9. Why the idea has sufficient scope 

10. What could be ready by December 

## **Final advice:** 

Do not choose the most fashionable idea. Choose the most defensible one. If you cannot clearly explain your contribution, your idea is not ready.
