## **FYP Idea Selection Fundamentals** 

Final Year Project guidance for 3-student FYP groups 

This document covers the conceptual rules students must understand before they begin serious ideation. It focuses on novelty, contribution, problem-first thinking, and the standards by which ideas should be judged before they are taken forward. 

## **1. What matters most at the start** 

At the beginning of FYP, the most important questions are: 

- **What problem are you solving?** 

- **What already exists?** 

- **What is still missing?** 

- **What exactly is your contribution?** 

- **Why is your idea not too similar to already completed FYPs?** 

- **Why is it large enough for a two-semester FYP, but still feasible?** 

## **Most important rule** 

## **Novelty is the primary filter.** 

_What new are you doing?_ 

If that answer is weak, vague, cosmetic, or based only on trendy technologies, the idea is weak even if a polished system could be built around it. 

## **2. What does not automatically count as novelty** 

The following are usually **not enough by themselves** : 

- We are using AI. 

- We are using LLMs. 

- We are using agents. 

- We are using an API. 

- We are building a chatbot. 

- This exists globally, but not locally. 

- We combined common features in one platform. 

- We improved the UI. 

- We changed it from web to mobile. 

- We switched one model to another. 

- We added voice support. 

- We added RAG. 

- We made it bilingual. 

These may describe the project, but they do **not** automatically define a meaningful contribution. 

## **3. Features are not the same as contribution** 

A long feature list does not make an FYP strong. 

These are usually **supporting features** , not the core contribution: 

- login/signup, 

- dashboards, 

- admin panels, 

- notifications, 

- reports, 

- profiles, 

- chat interfaces, 

- standard CRUD modules, 

- basic mobile/web frontends. 

Your FYP is not strong because it has many screens. It is strong if it has a **clear contribution** . 

## **4. A strong idea starts from a problem, not from a tool** 

## **Do not start from:** 

- Let’s build a chatbot. 

- Let’s use AI. 

- Let’s use agents. 

- Let’s use RAG. 

- Let’s build a platform. 

## **Start from:** 

- a real problem, 

- a broken or weak workflow, 

- an unmet need, 

- a neglected subproblem, 

- or an identifiable gap in current solutions. 

A useful framing is: 

**X users struggle with Y problem in Z context. Existing solutions solve part of the problem, but still fail in A, B, or C. Our project contributes D.** 

If you cannot frame your idea like this, it is probably not mature enough. 

## **5. Final rule for your group** 

Do not bring an idea forward because it sounds impressive, uses AI, or could become a polished product. 

Bring it forward only if you can clearly explain: 

- the problem, 

- the current state of solutions, 

- the real gap, 

- the exact contribution, 

- why it is not too similar to prior FYPs, 

- why it is not just a copy of existing products, 

- why it fits 3 students over 2 semesters, 

- and what meaningful proof of concept can exist by December. 

## **6. Final reminder** 

A polished implementation of a weakly novel idea is still risky. 

Always preserve this order of importance: 

1. **Novelty** 

2. **Contribution** 

3. **Scope** 

4. **Feasibility** 

5. **Polish** 

If the first two are weak, the rest will not save the project.
